# How to Record Tutorials, Online Classes and Interviews in Your Browser

A client needs to understand the design you just sent. Your students want to revisit an exercise. You have an interview planned, and your guest should only have to open a link.

Each situation calls for something similar: show what matters, explain it in your own voice and leave with a video you can save and share.

I'm building **Pling**, a free tool that lets you do this in a browser room without creating an account or installing an application. You can record alone or invite other people. This guide explains each workflow and what you will receive when you finish.

**Choose your task:** [record your screen and camera](https://pling.banegasn.dev/screen-and-camera-recorder), [record an online class](https://pling.banegasn.dev/record-online-classes) or [record a video interview](https://pling.banegasn.dev/video-interview-recorder).

## For consultants and freelancers: a tutorial your client can revisit

Imagine delivering a proposal, a spreadsheet or a new version of a website. You can accompany it with a video that walks through the material and explains your decisions.

Open a Pling room alone, enable your microphone and share the window you want to show. Keep your camera on if you want to appear in the video. In the recording studio, you can give the screen more space or change the composition during your explanation.

A simple structure for that tutorial:

1. Explain what the client will be able to do afterward.
2. Walk through one specific task, with screen text large enough to read.
3. Finish with the next step the client should take.

Make a short test before the full take. Play it back to check that your voice is audible and the screen details are readable in the saved video.

## For teachers: a lesson that remains useful after class

You can prepare an explanation without students present, or teach a live class inside Pling and record it. Each room supports **eight people in total**: for example, one teacher and up to seven students.

Share the slides, document or application you are teaching from. Agree to the recording with participants before you start, then confirm consent in the recording controls. The room shows an indicator while someone is recording.

During your explanation, add chapters with names such as “Worked example” or “Practice exercise”. The titles help you locate those moments afterward. If you take a break, pause recording while the call continues; paused time is left out of the saved file.

When you finish, save the video and share your copy through your learning platform or another service you choose. Pling does not automatically deliver the recording to students.

This workflow is for **classes hosted in Pling**. The tool does not join Zoom, Meet or Teams to record a session happening there.

## For creators: an interview with less setup for your guest

Create a room and send its link to the person you want to interview. Your guest enters a display name and allows camera and microphone access in their browser. No account is required.

Before the interview, do a short sound and recording check together. Headphones and a quiet space help. Explain how you plan to use the video and agree to record before starting.

Inside the studio, you can preview the composition, switch layouts and mark questions or answers you want to revisit later. Share your screen if the conversation involves a product or a portfolio.

Keep the output format in mind: Pling creates **one composed video with mixed audio**. If your editing workflow requires a separate audio track for each person or an original recording on each guest's device, that workflow is not currently available.

## What you see while recording and what you save afterward

The header screenshot shows the studio with demo participants. Its preview lets you check the composition being saved.

To begin, open the recording controls inside the room, choose your resolution, confirm consent and start the take. To save it, stop recording and use the browser’s save options.

Before recording, you can choose **720p or 1080p** output. Preview the result, change layouts, pause and resume, and add chapters. Chapter timestamps can also be exported separately.

The file is saved on your device in **WebM** format. Pling does not upload it or keep a copy of the video on its servers. You need to keep your own backup and decide where to share it. If you need MP4, convert the saved file with another compatible tool.

Chat and transcripts are handled separately from the video file; see [Pling's privacy policy](https://pling.banegasn.dev/legal/privacy) for details.

Pling does not impose a meeting timer. Your available storage, device performance and connection still affect what you can record. For screen sharing, use a supported desktop browser; the feature may be unavailable on mobile. Test with the equipment you intend to use, especially before an important session.
